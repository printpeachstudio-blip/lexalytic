2026-09-10
Most businesses I speak to use both Excel and accounting software.

But nobody has decided what lives where.

The result: the same number in two places. Manual transfers between systems. Reports nobody quite trusts because it is unclear which source is authoritative.

The fix is simpler than people expect.

Transaction data belongs in your accounting software. It is the authoritative record — what your accountant, HMRC, and any future acquirer will want to see.

Analysis, forecasting, and operational modelling belongs in Excel — drawing on data exported from accounting software rather than duplicating it.

One-way connection. Accounting software feeds Excel. Not the other way round.

Once that division is clear, the reconciliation problems stop.

2026-10-13
Knowing when to build custom software and when to buy off-the-shelf is one of the most important decisions a growing business makes.

Buy when: your process is standard. Your needs match what the software was built for. You want to be up and running in days, not weeks. The monthly cost is justified by the value.

Build when: your process is specific enough that generic tools require significant compromise. You have tried two or more off-the-shelf options and neither fits properly. The monthly licence cost over 3 years exceeds a custom build. You need something that integrates with systems the generic tools do not support.

The test is not "can we configure this tool to work?" Most tools can be configured to almost work.

The test is: "after configuration, are we still working around the tool or is the tool working for us?"

If the answer is still working around it — that is the signal.

2026-10-20
Automation is not always the answer.

Automating a broken process makes the breakage happen faster.

The conversation we have before any automation project starts is not "how do we automate this?" It is "is this the right process, or just the current one?"

A business that reconciles its bank accounts manually every month end does not need automated bank reconciliation. It needs to reconcile weekly so month end takes 30 minutes rather than 3 days. Then automate.

A business producing a weekly management report manually does not just need an automated report. It needs to ask whether the report answers the right questions, whether the data it pulls from is clean, and whether the people reading it are making decisions based on it.

Fix the process. Then automate it.

The technology is the easy part.
